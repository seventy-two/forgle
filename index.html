How to play

Every day there's a new commission: a sword, its steel and its size. Forge it, grind it, harden it and temper it, then it's tested.

Forge

  1. The billet starts as a plain bar, wider and shorter than the blade. Hammer each section down to the dashed outline; the steel you squeeze out of its width makes it longer.
  2. Pick a section by tapping it or its box below (or ← → and 1–8), then strike (Space). Strike when the marker crosses the centre of the anvil: a centred blow moves the most steel, an off-centre one less, and a glancing one stresses it. Each blow also moves the sections either side a little.
  3. Watch the colour. Hot steel moves easily; as it cools it moves less, which is how you make small corrections. The commission shows the colour not to hammer below: cold blows hardly move it and build cracks you won't see until the quench. Heat it in the fire (F) to heat it up, and take it out before it reaches the burning colour, where it throws sparks and scale eats it away.
  4. The boxes show how far each section is from its width: green is within 1%, a minus means you've hammered it too thin, and there's no making it wider again.
  5. You have 90 seconds. Widths are drawn taller than life so you can see them.
